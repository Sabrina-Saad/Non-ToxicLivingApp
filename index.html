🫒 The seed oil scale - from best to worst

  1. Best: beef tallow, ghee, butter, lard, coconut oil, extra-virgin olive oil, avocado oil
  2. Okay in a pinch: high-oleic sunflower or safflower oil (more stable, less omega-6), palm oil
  3. Avoid: canola/rapeseed, sunflower, safflower (regular), rice bran, grapeseed, sesame (flavoring amounts are minor)
  4. Worst: soybean, corn, cottonseed, "vegetable oil", and anything hydrogenated or interesterified

Tip: ingredients are listed by weight. If a seed oil is the last ingredient, it's a small amount - that's the "least bad" choice when nothing clean exists.

🥩 How to buy real beef from a farmer

  1. Ask: "Is it 100% grass-fed AND grass-finished?" Many "grass-fed" cattle are grain-finished.
  2. Ask: "Were the animals born and raised on your farm or in the US?"
  3. Ask: "Any growth hormones, routine antibiotics, or grain feed?" A good farmer happily explains their practices.
  4. Buy in bulk: a quarter cow (~100 lbs of meat) fits in a chest freezer and is often cheaper per pound than grocery-store grass-fed.
  5. In stores, look for: "Product of USA" (since Jan 1, 2026 it must mean born, raised, slaughtered & processed in the US), American Grassfed certification, or the farm name on the package.

Fluoride & your pineal gland

Your pineal gland is a tiny gland in the center of your brain that makes melatonin - the hormone that runs your sleep cycle. In 2001, researcher Jennifer Luke found that fluoride accumulates in the pineal gland - at higher concentrations than in bone - as it calcifies with age [11]. Her earlier animal research found fluoride lowered melatonin output in gerbils.

In 2024 the US government's own National Toxicology Program concluded, with moderate confidence, that fluoride exposure above 1.5 mg/L is associated with lower IQ in children [12]. That same year a federal judge ordered the EPA to take regulatory action on fluoride in drinking water. In 2025, Utah and Florida became the first states to ban adding fluoride to public water.

📌 Being straight with you: US water is fluoridated at 0.7 mg/L, and the NTP said there wasn't enough data to know whether that lower level affects IQ. The pineal research shows fluoride builds up there - whether that harms sleep or health in humans hasn't been proven yet. We think "not proven safe" is reason enough to choose a fluoride-free toothpaste and a reverse-osmosis filter.

Red 40 and our kids

In 2007, a randomized, double-blind study published in The Lancet (the "Southampton study") gave children drinks containing common food dyes plus sodium benzoate. Both 3-year-olds and 8-9-year-olds became measurably more hyperactive [13]. That study is why Europe added the warning label.

In 2021, California's Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment reviewed the evidence and concluded synthetic food dyes can cause or worsen neurobehavioral problems - like inattention and hyperactivity - in some children [14]. California has since banned Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6 and other dyes from school food [15], and in 2025 the FDA announced a plan to phase petroleum-based dyes out of the US food supply - after decades of parents being told they were "safe".

Testosterone, fertility & the chemicals around us

American men have less testosterone than men the same age did a generation ago - a large study found levels falling by roughly 1% a year, independent of age [16]. Global sperm counts have dropped by more than 50% since the 1970s [17].

The best-studied culprits are endocrine disruptors - chemicals that mimic or block hormones - like phthalates and BPA in plastics, synthetic fragrance and food packaging. That's why this app cares about what's in your food and what touches your skin.

📌 Being straight with you: we haven't found a human study proving Red 40 itself lowers testosterone. Red 40's documented harm is to kids' behavior. Plastics and hormone-disrupting chemicals are the bigger, better-proven threat to hormones - and polyester is plastic.

Polyester is plastic. You're wearing it.

In the early 1990s, Egyptian urologist Ahmed Shafik had men wear polyester slings against the scrotum. Within months, sperm counts dropped to zero - he literally tested it as a male contraceptive. After they stopped wearing polyester, sperm production returned to normal [18]. His studies in dogs found the same effect, which he linked to the electrostatic charge polyester creates against the skin.

Since then, scientists have found microplastics in human placentas [19] and in every human testicle sample tested in a 2024 study [20]. Polyester clothing sheds microfibers every time you wear and wash it, and in 2022 the Center for Environmental Health found BPA in polyester-spandex socks from major brands. Documentaries have pushed this into the mainstream - couples swapping polyester bedding and underwear for natural fibers - and the research behind it goes back decades.

📌 Being straight with you: Shafik's studies were small and haven't been repeated at scale. But natural fibers have no downside - so why take the risk? See the Clothing tab.

It's not just your waistline

A 2024 umbrella review in the BMJ, pooling 45 analyses covering nearly 10 million people, found ultra-processed food is linked to higher risk of heart disease death, type 2 diabetes, anxiety and depression [21]. Europe has its own problems - but its people eat far less ultra-processed food, and it shows.
